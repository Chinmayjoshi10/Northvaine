## Goal
The heart of rule #2: decide, in code first, whether a proposed claim's quote really is in its
document, then let a fast model confirm only that the quote supports the statement.

## Contract
`radar/quote_check.py` (`normalize_text`, `find_quote`, `quote_contains`) and `radar/verify.py`
(`is_evidence_document`, `claim_fingerprint`, `verify_claims`) as frozen in PLAN.md. `SupportOutput`
is T4.0's and must not change.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] `normalize_text`: NFKC, curly quotes to straight, all dash variants to `-`, whitespace runs
      to one space, stripped. `find_quote(mode=extracted)`: quotes shorter than `quote_min_chars`
      (after normalization) give `found=False, reason=quote_too_short`; then exact match on the
      normalized text; otherwise a stdlib `difflib` sliding-window fallback at ratio >=
      `quote_match_min_ratio`. `offset` indexes the ORIGINAL text and
      `text[offset:offset+length]` is the matched span. Tests: a re-wrapped, re-quoted quote; a
      paraphrase (rejected); a quote from a different document (rejected); unicode (NFKC, curly
      quotes, en dash); a near miss just under and just over 0.95.
- [ ] `find_quote(mode=structured)`: found only if the quote equals one whole line of the text
      byte for byte; no normalization, no fuzzy, no minimum length; `method='line'` and the offset
      is the line start. Tests: `CPV: 45233000` and `Buyer country: DEU` found; a line prefix,
      a substring inside a line, and a re-spaced line are all rejected.
- [ ] `quote_contains(quote, fragment)`: normalized, casefolded substring test; empty fragment is
      False. Tested.
- [ ] `is_evidence_document` follows `evidence_source_rules` and is fail-closed: `ted` with
      `meta.schema_ver == 1` -> structured; `website` -> full_text; `tavily` with
      `meta.text_kind == 'raw'` -> full_text; `tavily` snippet, any unknown text_kind, a `ted`
      document without `schema_ver` 1, and any unknown source -> lead with a reason. Tested on
      parsed Tavily fixture documents of both kinds.
- [ ] `verify_claims` runs the quote check on every draft first. Lead documents give every draft
      `ineligible_document`; `mode=structured` on a non-structured document raises ValueError. Only
      quote-passing drafts go into ONE `claim_support` call per document (fast tier,
      `support_max_tokens`), whose prompt receives the statements and quotes only, fenced as
      untrusted, with no document text and no case goal. With a recording fake client under
      `client_override`, tests assert: a failed quote never appears in any request body; no LLM call
      is made when no quote passes or when `support=False`.
- [ ] Statuses: `verified` (`verified_by='code+llm'`, or `'code'` when `support=False`),
      `quote_not_found`, `quote_too_short`, `unsupported` (the LLM said no, or returned no verdict
      for that index, or a duplicate/out-of-range index: fail-closed), `ineligible_document`.
      `quote_offset` is set for every quote-passing check. `claim_fingerprint` is sha256 over
      (document_id, normalized statement, source_quote) and deterministic. Tested.
- [ ] `claim_support.v1.md` has front matter, declares its untrusted variables, tells the model the
      fenced text is data, and contains no industry words (`check_invariants` passes).

## Invariants touched
- #2: code check before any LLM, offsets into the original text, fail-closed statuses.
- #7: statements (LLM-written) and quotes are fenced; the support check sees no document or goal.
- #8: one `complete_json` call through `llm.py`; tests use `client_override`, never the SDK.

## Notes
- The difflib fallback must stay bounded: compare windows of the quote's length (+/- 10%) and
  skip it when the text is over `extract_max_doc_chars` x 2; exact match covers the common case.
- Prompt wording is free, but every change after recording costs a re-record (D53).
